\section{Recommendations and Limitations}\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Candidate practices for similar projects}

The case study supports four candidate practices. They are design
recommendations grounded in observed failures and successful detections,
not estimates
of causal effectiveness. % CLAIM: CLM-005, CLM-009

\emph{Treat the public statement as a proof obligation.}
Elaboration establishes that a proposition is well formed; it does not
establish that the proposition expresses the intended mathematics.
Statement review should therefore include scope comparison, boundary
cases, and counterexample search before proof work is accepted. The
paired statement incidents in Section~\ref{sec:incidents} show one
contrast: Case B was corrected before merge, whereas Case A remained
unreviewed until the pre-release audit. % CLAIM: CLM-005, INC-001, INC-002

\emph{Assign one control to each artifact at risk.}
The typed theorem, its proof assumptions, the public declaration set,
and the release import surface are distinct artifacts. Semantic review,
axiom inspection, declaration comparison, and release attestation
observe different properties; success in one is not evidence that the
others were checked. % CLAIM: CLM-009, INC-001, INC-004

\emph{Use an issue and pull request as the durable unit of work.}
A bounded issue records scope and acceptance criteria. An isolated
worktree gives one implementing agent ownership of the candidate, and
the pull request records the committed diff, validation results, and an
durable independent review record. Status reports remain provisional until
the committed diff and validation outputs are rechecked.
% CLAIM: CLM-007, CLM-009, EV-2076, EV-1669

\emph{Include operational state in the harness contract.}
Liveness, process cleanup, build serialization, cache reuse, and
available-memory checks affect whether a candidate can be reproduced and
reviewed. Monitoring the complete process tree and resource headroom is
more informative than inspecting the visible Lean process alone.
Section~\ref{sec:incidents} provides one observed resource failure, not
a benchmark of the proposed controls.
% CLAIM: CLM-008, CLM-009, INC-005

\subsection{Evidence and limits}

The campaign covered one project under one scientific owner. Six
sessions were selected purposively for event coding, so their counts
describe that sample and cannot estimate failure prevalence or guardrail
effectiveness. There is no matched campaign without agent orchestration,
and the observations do not identify causal effects.
% CLAIM: CLM-004, CLM-005

The session record also has coverage gaps, including an early loss caused
by log retention. Reconstructed events are marked separately from
primary session evidence and carry an explicit confidence assessment.
The models and proprietary tools changed outside the project's control,
which limits behavioral reproducibility. Tool-specific mechanisms are
reported as implementation details; the candidate practices above are
phrased in terms of artifacts and responsibilities that do not require
the same software. % CLAIM: CLM-008, INC-003

The scientific owner's supervision was part of the observed harness and
cannot be separated from its outcome. The mathematical result is also
bounded by Section~\ref{sec:formalization}: homogeneous equations, an
arbitrary fixed finite horizon, separated-variable test functions, and
the stated energy class. The paper makes no claim about uniqueness or
higher regularity.

Redacted evidence excerpts, claim-to-evidence manifests, and scripts for
the reported metrics are available in the paper repository. Raw session
logs are withheld to protect the people and systems recorded in them.
