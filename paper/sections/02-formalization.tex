\section{Formalization Target and Verified Results}\label{sec:formalization}

The target is the classical existence theorem for
Leray--Hopf weak solutions to the unforced, incompressible
Navier--Stokes equations~\cite{leray1934,hopf1951}: given a
divergence-free initial datum $u_0 \in L^2$, a viscosity $\nu > 0$, and a
time horizon $T > 0$, a weak solution on $[0,T]$ exists. The two domains
formalized here are the periodic unit 3-torus $\mathbb{T}^3$ and the whole
space $\mathbb{R}^3$. All statements below are
quoted from commit \texttt{7c15710a}, tagged
\texttt{v0.1.0-rc1}~\cite{lerayhopf2026repo}, whose scope statement defines
the result reported here. % CLAIM: CLM-001 (leray-hopf@7c15710a7b9068a2aa105fc7c11b432e7685b7b5)

\subsection{Weak solutions as formalized}

A weak solution is represented by a velocity field
$u : \mathrm{Time} \to D_\sigma$, where $D_\sigma$ is the closed subspace of
divergence-free fields in $L^2$ of the respective domain (Lean types
\texttt{L2Sigma} and \texttt{L2Sigma\_R3}), together with proofs of the
properties in Table~\ref{tab:solution-fields}. The abbreviations for the two domains are
\texttt{LerayHopfSolutionFull} on $\mathbb{T}^3$ and
\texttt{LerayHopfSolutionFull\_R3} on $\mathbb{R}^3$. Both instantiate
the common \texttt{Galerkin.LerayHopfSolution} structure.

The weak formulation is restricted to test functions separated in time and space,
$\psi(t)w(x)$. General tests $\varphi(t,x)$ that depend jointly on time
and space are outside the formalized relation. The initial trace is the one-sided strong limit
$u(t)\to u_0$ in the ambient $L^2$ norm as $t\to0^+$; weak continuity on
the whole interval is not asserted.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{Fields in the formalized weak solution.}\label{tab:solution-fields}
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.25\linewidth}p{0.65\linewidth}@{}}
\toprule
Component & What is guaranteed \\
\midrule
Velocity field & valued in the closed divergence-free subspace $D_\sigma$ at every $t$ \\
Weak formulation & Navier--Stokes identity against tests $\psi(t)w(x)$ separated in time and space, with no forcing term \\
Energy inequality & $\tfrac12\|u(t)\|^2 + \int_0^t \mathrm{dissip}(\nu,u(s))\,ds \le \tfrac12\|u_0\|^2$ for $t\in[0,T]$ \\

Measurability & strong measurability almost everywhere of $t\mapsto u(t)$ into the ambient $L^2$ space \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\subsection{Capstone existence theorems}

The two capstone statements are quoted verbatim from the pinned commit
(proof bodies elided):

\begin{lstlisting}
-- LerayHopf/Torus/GalerkinODECapstone.lean  (T^3)
theorem exists_lerayHopf_torus3 (u₀ : L2Sigma) (ν : ℝ) (hν : 0 < ν)
    (T : ℝ) (hT : 0 < T) :
    ∃ F : Torus3NSForms, Nonempty (LerayHopfSolutionFull F ν T u₀)

-- LerayHopf/R3/GalerkinODECapstone.lean     (R^3)
theorem exists_lerayHopf_r3 (u₀ : L2Sigma_R3) (ν : ℝ) (hν : 0 < ν)
    (T : ℝ) (hT : 0 < T) :
    ∃ (𝔊 : R3GalerkinScheme) (F : R3NSForms 𝔊),
    Nonempty (LerayHopfSolutionFull_R3 𝔊 F ν T u₀)
\end{lstlisting}
% CLAIM: CLM-001 (decl:exists_lerayHopf_torus3, decl:exists_lerayHopf_r3)

Within the stated domain, both quantify over \emph{every} divergence-free
$u_0$, every viscosity $\nu>0$, and every horizon $T>0$. On
$\mathbb{R}^3$ the
witness scheme $\mathfrak{G}$ (a concrete Galerkin scheme built from a
Schwartz divergence-free basis) is itself produced by the proof, not
assumed.

The result establishes existence for the unforced equation on an arbitrary
fixed finite horizon, under the weak formulation restricted to separated
test functions and the energy class stated above; it does not address
uniqueness, nonuniqueness, or higher regularity. Developments outside this
scope are kept in an experimental namespace that neither capstone imports
(Section~\ref{sec:release-surface}).

\subsection{Main analytic difficulties}

At the pinned commit, the formalization contains 96 files and about
42,000 lines.
% CLAIM: CLM-001 (formalization-metrics.json, measured at leray-hopf@7c15710a)
It is organized around three analytic tasks shared by both domains.

\emph{Galerkin approximation.}
On each finite-dimensional subspace $V_n$, the truncated quadratic
vector field is $C^1$ and satisfies a dissipative estimate for its inner
product with the state. Forward global existence is assembled by gluing local
Picard--Lindelöf solutions.

\emph{Spatial compactness.}
The torus proof uses a Rellich argument based on the tail of the Fourier
modes. The $\mathbb{R}^3$ proof uses a local Rellich--Kondrachov
embedding based on a Fréchet--Kolmogorov criterion.

\emph{Time compactness and limit passage.}
A shared evolution-triple and weak limit layer supports the
domain-specific Aubin--Lions arguments: a spectral decomposition by mode
on $\mathbb{T}^3$ and step-curve total boundedness in time on
$\mathbb{R}^3$. The ambient infrastructure --- $L^p$
spaces and the Bochner integral, Hilbert-space duality and projection,
Fourier analysis, and Picard--Lindel\"of ODE theory --- comes from
mathlib, while the
evolution triple, the Aubin--Lions and weak limit packages, the Rellich
and Fr\'echet--Kolmogorov compactness arguments, and the trilinear
convection extension are defined and proved in the project; the pinned
mathlib has no evolution-triple, Aubin--Lions, or Bochner--Sobolev-in-time
theory. The nonlinear convection term is represented through that
trilinear extension with explicit proofs for the Schwartz/Galerkin test
class, rather than by postulating a bounded operator on
$L^2\times L^2\times L^2$.

\subsection{Kernel axioms and public imports}\label{sec:release-surface}

For both capstones, \texttt{\#print axioms} reports exactly
\texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice}, and \texttt{Quot.sound}, so
neither depends on an axiom declared by the project or on
\texttt{sorryAx}. By release preparation, the PR workflow ran textual
guards for \texttt{sorry} and the public import closure, and a manually
dispatched full build recorded this axiom list from the live declarations
at the pinned commit. % CLAIM: CLM-001

Both capstones are existence propositions: each concludes in a
\texttt{Nonempty} wrapper under an existential quantifier, so neither is a
term intended for evaluation, and the Galerkin sequence and package
builders they invoke are declared \texttt{noncomputable}. Reporting no
project axioms therefore does not make the development constructive:
\texttt{Classical.choice} is in the recorded axiom list of both theorems.
What the paper reports is a machine-checked classical existence theorem,
not an executable PDE solver, and no claim is made about extracting a
computable weak solution from it. % CLAIM: CLM-001
