\section{Failures, Incidents, and Recovery}\label{sec:incidents}

% Scope (PLAN.md §7.4): representative examples from mathematical-meaning errors,
% false success reports, resource exhaustion, orchestration failures.
% Each incident presented here must have a card evidence/incidents/INC-NNN.md
% with confidence-of-reconstruction stated. Reference as: % CLAIM: INC-NNN

Neither a green build nor a kernel-level axiom check is sufficient to
guarantee that a formalization campaign is proceeding soundly: a build
verifies that whatever is currently stated type-checks, and an axiom check
verifies that whatever is currently proved rests on no unexpected
assumptions, but neither mechanism inspects whether an unproved placeholder's
\emph{statement} is true, nor whether a declaration silently disappeared, nor
whether the host running the campaign has enough memory left to finish. This
section examines four incidents in which exactly this kind of gap opened up,
what mechanism outside the build eventually closed it, and what standing
guardrail was left behind. A fifth coded failure mode --- the loss of the
project's earliest session logs to a default log-retention setting --- is a
research-record integrity problem rather than a formalization failure and is
treated with the paper's evidence-coverage limitations elsewhere rather than
here. % CLAIM: INC-003 (forward reference only)

\subsection{Descriptive statistics from the coded sessions}

Six sessions spanning the campaign were independently event-coded against a
fixed vocabulary (statement-intent mismatches, theorem weakenings,
mathematical errors, resource exhaustion, worktree conflicts, handoff
failures, model-escalation failures, proof-engineering deadlocks, successful
recoveries, and false success reports, where the last denotes a run-time
build-success claim that is contradicted by on-disk state and is never
corrected within the session). Table~\ref{tab:event-codes} reports the
counts relevant to this section; the remaining four codes not broken out
there occurred as follows: handoff failures 4, model-escalation failures 2,
mathematical errors 2, and a proof-engineering deadlock 1. % CLAIM: CLM-004
These six sessions were chosen purposively --- to cover the campaign's
distinct phases and hosts, not by random or exhaustive sampling of the full
campaign --- so the counts describe only this sample and must not be read as
a prevalence estimate, a failure rate, or a measurement of guardrail
effectiveness. % CLAIM: CLM-004
In particular, no false success report was observed in any coded session;
this is consistent with a discipline, present from the earliest coded
session onward, of re-verifying on-disk state before trusting a sub-agent's
progress report and before committing, rather than a claim that this
discipline caused the absence. % CLAIM: CLM-004

\subsection{A false generalization that survived to pre-release review}
\label{sec:inc001}

The most consequential incident concerns a single public declaration,
\texttt{w1pTime\_continuous\_in\_H}, introduced with an honest \texttt{sorry}
and a generic hypothesis pair (exponents $p,q \geq 1$) as part of a
multi-file scaffold. % CLAIM: INC-001
The only proof attempt ever actually carried out for this declaration
assumed the special case $p=q=2$, where the relevant energy-pairing
limit-passage argument is available via Cauchy--Schwarz; the public
signature, however, was never narrowed to match, and continued to assert the
general statement. That general statement is false: a pre-release soundness
review constructed an explicit counterexample at $p=q=1$, using a weighted
Hilbert-triple with a spike sequence, showing that the hypotheses admit
inputs for which no continuous representative exists. % CLAIM: INC-001
Between the scaffold's introduction and that review, roughly a month
elapsed, reconstructed from commit records since the introducing session
itself was not identified among the collected logs. % CLAIM: INC-001
A mid-campaign session did stage a multi-hour direct attack on this
declaration, but after running into a circular proof route it closed the
investigation on the basis of a one-minute check that the declaration was
unreferenced by any other proof, not on any check of the statement's truth
--- so the false generalization survived a direct engagement with the
declaration without being caught. % CLAIM: INC-001
The declaration was never on the critical path of either capstone theorem,
so the machine-checked, axiom-free proof was never at risk; but the false
statement was carried, restated with increasing confidence, into adjoining
design documents and a companion notes corpus before detection.

The pre-release review that finally caught the error named eleven
recurring anti-patterns behind it; five of the most transferable are worth
naming individually. An \emph{honest-\texttt{sorry} does not imply a true
statement} fallacy treated the placeholder's visible honesty about being
unproved as a substitute for checking that what it asserted was even
correct. A \emph{special-case-proof, general-signature} mismatch let a
public type stay broader than anything actually established for it.
\emph{Dead-declaration rot} meant that because the theorem was unused, no
downstream consumer's own review would ever have surfaced the defect, so its
statement went essentially unaudited for the same reason it was harmless.
\emph{Self-referential verification} in the companion notes corpus re-derived
its confidence from the same source docstring it was supposed to be checking
independently, rather than from the Lean statement or the literature.
Finally, \emph{large-batch review dilution} meant that when many scaffolded
declarations were reviewed together, the density of attention on any one
statement dropped. % CLAIM: INC-001
The recovery narrowed the declaration to the case actually proved, split it
into an experimental namespace, and introduced a standing regression guard
(checked in continuous integration) against two concrete violations:
reintroduction of the general signature, and any placeholder declaration
missing an accompanying statement card that records its adjudicated scope
and required adversarial substitutions. % CLAIM: INC-001
Tellingly, two days after that guardrail work merged, a follow-up pull
request touching the same files' documentation was merged with no requested
or submitted independent review at all, reproducing --- inside the very
postmortem's aftermath --- the same class of unreviewed-merge handoff
failure the postmortem had just been written to prevent; a subsequent audit
caught two residual inaccuracies from that merge and closed the loop with a
properly reviewed follow-up. % CLAIM: INC-001
That recurrence is itself evidence that adopting a policy is not the same as
the policy holding on the next low-priority pull request.

\subsection{A contrast: the same blind spot caught before merge}

A structurally identical blind spot --- an unproved placeholder standing in
for a proposition whose truth had never actually been established, sitting
inside two abandoned proof attempts --- arose earlier in the campaign in a
different theorem, a box-truncated antisymmetry property of a convection
form on the three-torus. % CLAIM: INC-002
Here, an independent adjudicator reviewing the pull request before merge
constructed a numerical counterexample for the unconstrained case (a
discrepancy of roughly 80.7) and confirmed that the property held, to
numerical precision, only once the inputs were restricted to a
finite-dimensional projected subspace. The author acknowledged that both
prior attempts had in fact been unsound. The signature was narrowed to the
provable, restricted case, the proof completed, and the placeholder removed
and merged the same day: detection to merge took about sixteen minutes.
% CLAIM: INC-002
Neither the compiler nor the project's syntactic sorry/axiom checks
distinguished this false placeholder from a true one; only the adversarial,
numerically-grounded review did. Read together with the previous case, this
is a contrast --- one instance of the same blind spot reaching a pre-release
soundness review after roughly a month, the other caught before merge on the
same day it was introduced --- consistent with the value of independent
statement review backed by concrete counterexample testing; it does not
establish that such review would have caught the first case had it been
applied there, since the two declarations were not reviewed under identical
conditions. % CLAIM: CLM-005

\subsection{A short example: silent deletion caught by declaration-level review}

A large deduplication refactor extracted duplicated tensor and edge-gluing
code from two files into a shared module and rewired the original public
declarations as thin instantiations. In that rewiring, one public theorem,
\texttt{edge\_inf\_eq\_schwartz\_tensor}, was silently dropped from the list
of declarations to re-wire and disappeared from the file entirely, while
neighboring public declarations were correctly preserved. % CLAIM: INC-004
Neither the build nor an earlier structural review of the refactor's module
boundaries caught this, because nothing downstream referenced the deleted
theorem and both checks operate above the level of individual declarations.
Detection came from a per-declaration, byte-level comparison against the
pre-refactor file, carried out by a reviewer that spawned one verification
sub-agent per pull request rather than reviewing the batch of twelve
refactor pull requests serially; the missing theorem was restored, verified
byte-identical to its pre-refactor statement, and the pull request merged
within roughly four and a half minutes of detection. % CLAIM: INC-004
The general point survives the small scale of this instance: build success
does not guarantee that a public theorem still exists, and only a
declaration-level, exhaustive comparison against the prior state catches its
quiet removal.

\subsection{A resource case: side-process accumulation and a memory-exhaustion cascade}
\label{sec:inc005}

The fourth incident is not a soundness failure but a resource-management
one, arising in a memory-constrained container (a hard cgroup ceiling under
3.5\,GiB) running a continuous multi-worktree orchestration. % CLAIM: INC-005
A prior night's wave of per-worktree code-review invocations had each left
behind a broker/application-server side-process chain that is not reclaimed
automatically; by the next morning fourteen such stale, orphaned chains
(traceable to worktrees that had since been merged and deleted) had
accumulated across roughly one to 1.5\,GB. Independently, an orchestrator
misjudgment --- treating a slow-responding prior agent as dead and
dispatching a successor into the same worktree, when the prior agent was in
fact still alive --- produced several hours of duplicated work, and the
successor, unaware of a hard-linked shared build-cache convention, began a
cold rebuild of the entire mathlib dependency from source. % CLAIM: INC-005
The combined load produced repeated silent build deaths near the end of the
heaviest compilation step, indistinguishable at first from ordinary build
failures; routine progress checks that looked only at the visible Lean
process's memory use reported ample headroom, missing both the accumulated
side-process chains and the shrinking system-wide available memory.
Detection was triggered not by the orchestration's own monitoring but by an
incidental owner status check, after which the available-memory figure (as
opposed to used-memory) was checked directly, the stale chains were
identified by tracing each process's working directory against currently
live worktrees, killed in one pass, and the kernel's out-of-memory counter
was read for the first time --- confirming retrospectively that every prior
silent build death had in fact been a kernel out-of-memory kill, though the
counter is cumulative since container start and so does not itself attribute
which kill happened when. % CLAIM: INC-005
Recovery restored available memory, and a rebuild under a single global
build lock succeeded.

Two further points make this incident more than a one-off cleanup. First,
the underlying lesson --- that a tool's own long-running helper processes
must be reclaimed structurally, not left to chance --- had already been
learned once before, in an unrelated project, after a comparable
process-accumulation cascade; because that lesson had been recorded only in
project-scoped memory rather than generalized across projects, it was not
applied here until the same failure class recurred in a different concrete
form. The standing guardrails written afterward were deliberately placed
in a project-independent location for exactly this reason. Second, the
mitigation targeted the path that had actually caused the cascade
(accumulated side-process chains), and within the same day a second,
structurally different low-memory episode occurred --- this time the
legitimate peak memory use of a single heavy compilation, with no
accumulated chains and no change in the out-of-memory counter. Because the
guardrails also included a symptom-based rule (report whenever available
memory drops below a fixed threshold, regardless of cause), this second
episode was self-reported and confirmed harmless without incident, whereas
a narrower guardrail aimed only at the original cause would have missed it.
% CLAIM: INC-005
The general point is that in a memory-constrained, agent-orchestrated Lean
development setting, resource management is not a peripheral operational
concern but part of orchestration itself, and monitoring built around
symptoms generalizes further than monitoring built around a single
identified cause.

\subsection{What the incidents show together}

Across these four cases, the standing discipline that now governs the
project's formalization workflow was in every instance a response to a
specific, dated failure rather than a design anticipated in advance: the
statement-gate policy and its regression guard followed the false
generalization of \S\ref{sec:inc001}; the practice of exhaustive
declaration-level byte comparison on large refactors followed the silent
deletion; and the resource guardrails followed the memory-exhaustion
cascade. What is transferable across them is not any single fix but three
detection mechanisms that generalize beyond this project: independent
statement review backed by concrete counterexample construction, exhaustive
declaration-level comparison against a prior known-good state, and
symptom-based (rather than single-cause-based) resource monitoring. The
recurrence inside \S\ref{sec:inc001} of an unreviewed merge two days after
the very postmortem meant to prevent it, and the recurrence inside
\S\ref{sec:inc005} of a
structurally different low-memory episode the same afternoon the targeted
fix landed, both indicate that adopting a guardrail is a beginning, not a
guarantee, and that the discipline observed in this project is itself
incident-driven and still failure-prone at the point a new guardrail is
introduced.
