\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

Large language model agents increasingly work on formalizations that span
weeks rather than a single proof search. Such a project, divided among
many GitHub Issues, branches, agents, and reviews, must do more than obtain proofs
the kernel accepts: it must decide which type should be proved, preserve
public declarations during refactoring, and verify the operating state
behind progress reports.

This paper studies those obligations through six weeks of work on the
existence of Leray--Hopf weak solutions to the unforced incompressible
Navier--Stokes equations. The central artifact is a
Lean~4~\cite{moura2021lean4} development on
mathlib~\cite{mathlib2020}, with capstone existence theorems on the
three dimensional torus and on $\mathbb{R}^3$~\cite{leray1934,hopf1951,
lerayhopf2026repo}. % CLAIM: CLM-001
The theorem is classical; the mathematical contribution is its
machine-checked formalization within the scope stated in
Section~\ref{sec:formalization}.
To our knowledge, no prior machine-checked formalization establishes
the existence of Leray--Hopf weak solutions to the three-dimensional
Navier--Stokes equations. % CLAIM: CLM-006
In September 2026, OpenAI released a Lean formalization of a finite time
blow-up construction for the forced three-dimensional Navier--Stokes
equations, addressing alternatives C and D of the Clay
formulation~\cite{openai2026navierstokes}. That
result is separate from the unforced Leray--Hopf existence theorem
formalized here.

The nearest results in formalized analysis address adjacent problems: the
Gagliardo--Nirenberg--Sobolev inequality in
mathlib~\cite{vandoorn2024gns}, a Lean formalization of
De Giorgi--Nash--Moser elliptic regularity
theory~\cite{armstrong2026degiorginashmoser}, and a formalization of
well-posedness and the mean-field derivation for the nonlinear Vlasov
equation, conducted with AI assistance~\cite{miller2026vlasov}.

Large collaborative formalizations offer a second comparison. Human teams
coordinated through blueprints in the Odd Order Theorem and the Liquid
Tensor Experiment~\cite{gonthier2013oddorder, commelin2023abstraction}.
TauCeti uses human governance and a standing review rubric for a Lean
library written by AI~\cite{tauceti2026}. % CLAIM: CLM-010

The closest engineering comparison is
LeanMarathon~\cite{zhang2026leanmarathon}, a multi-agent harness for
long-horizon Lean autoformalization. It coordinates four fixed agent
roles (Blueprinter, Target-Reviewer, Worker, and Refiner) through an
explicit blueprint that serves as proof skeleton, proof directed acyclic
graph (DAG), and shared record. It discharges the DAG from its leaves in
CI-gated rounds. The present project instead coordinated a six-week
formalization mainly through conventional development artifacts (Issues,
worktrees, PRs, reviews, CI, and the evolving Lean repository), without
an explicit proof DAG, a blueprint language, or fixed mathematics-specific
agent roles. The dependency structure of the mathematics was carried by
the Lean sources and the author's Issue scoping.

The present study examines one deep formalization under a single
author and retains evidence about its incidents and agent sessions.
Agentic theorem proving systems are commonly evaluated through proof
search or benchmarks~\cite{yang2023leandojo,
deepmind2025alphaproof}. Studies of autonomous task
horizons and pull requests (PRs) written by agents motivate reliability
questions~\cite{metr2025longtasks,agenticprs2026security}.
Lean Eval fixes the statement, the supplied dependencies, and the trusted
scaffolding in a generated workspace, then scores whether a comparator
accepts the submitted solution~\cite{leaneval2026repo,
leaneval2026site}. % CLAIM: CLM-011
Our unit of analysis is instead a repository-level formalization
campaign whose preserved records trace changes from Issue definition
through review and mechanical checks, and document incidents and
resource use.

This paper makes three contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item A completed Lean~4 and mathlib formalization, with no project
  axioms, of the classical existence theorem for Leray--Hopf weak
  solutions on $\mathbb{T}^3$ and $\mathbb{R}^3$, whose exact scope is
  independently inspectable at a pinned release.
\item An empirical account of the six-week agent-driven development. The
  author's harness connected a conventional Issue, worktree, PR, and
  review workflow with specialist agents and a constrained runtime,
  selecting checks by the changed artifact. Four documented incidents show
  those checks' observed reach and limits.
  % CLAIM: CLM-005, CLM-007, CLM-008, CLM-009
\item An evidence method combining retained session logs, claim-to-evidence
  verification, and cost reconciliation, which supports auditable
  statements about the project, including token use and cost estimates,
  while making coverage gaps explicit
  (Section~\ref{sec:workflow}).
  % CLAIM: CLM-002, CLM-003
\end{enumerate}
