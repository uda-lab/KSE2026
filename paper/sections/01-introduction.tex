\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

Large language model agents increasingly work on formalizations that span
weeks rather than a single proof search. A project divided among many
GitHub Issues, branches, agents, and reviews must preserve mathematical
intent as well as proofs accepted by the kernel. The kernel checks a
declaration against its type. The project must also decide which type
should be proved, preserve the intended public declarations during
refactoring, and verify the operating state behind progress reports.

This paper studies those obligations through six weeks of work on
Leray--Hopf weak solution existence for the unforced incompressible
Navier--Stokes equations. The central artifact is a
Lean~4~\cite{moura2021lean4} development on
mathlib~\cite{mathlib2020}, with capstone existence theorems on the
three dimensional torus and on $\mathbb{R}^3$~\cite{leray1934,hopf1951,
lerayhopf2026repo}. Both theorem statements are given precisely in
Section~\ref{sec:formalization}. % CLAIM: CLM-001
To our knowledge, no prior machine-checked formalization establishes
Leray--Hopf weak solution existence for the three-dimensional
Navier--Stokes equations. % CLAIM: CLM-006

The nearest results in formalized analysis address adjacent problems.
They include the
Gagliardo--Nirenberg--Sobolev inequality in
mathlib~\cite{vandoorn2024gns}, a Lean formalization of
De Giorgi--Nash--Moser elliptic regularity
theory~\cite{armstrong2026degiorginashmoser}, and a formalization of
well-posedness and the mean-field derivation for the nonlinear Vlasov
equation, conducted with AI assistance~\cite{miller2026vlasov}.

Large collaborative formalization projects provide a second comparison.
Human teams coordinated through blueprints in the Odd Order Theorem and
the Liquid Tensor Experiment~\cite{gonthier2013oddorder,
commelin2023abstraction}.
TauCeti uses human governance and a standing review rubric for
a Lean library written by AI~\cite{tauceti2026}. % CLAIM: CLM-010
The present study instead
examines one deep formalization under a single scientific owner and
retains evidence about its incidents and agent sessions.

Agentic theorem proving systems are commonly evaluated through proof
search, benchmarks, or interactive assistance~\cite{xin2025deepseekproverv2,
yang2023leandojo,
deepmind2025alphaproof,zhou2026leanatlas}. Studies of autonomous task
horizons and pull requests written by agents motivate reliability
questions~\cite{metr2025longtasks,agenticprs2026security}.
Our unit of analysis is different: a complete mathematical project whose
implementation, reviews, incidents, and resource use can be traced to
preserved artifacts.

This paper makes three contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item A completed Lean~4 and mathlib formalization, with no project
  axioms, of Leray--Hopf weak solution existence on $\mathbb{T}^3$ and
  $\mathbb{R}^3$. The formal result and its exact scope are independently
  inspectable.
\item A harness defined by the owner that connects GitHub Issues, Git worktrees,
  pull requests, specialist agents, and the constrained runtime
  environment. Issue scope, review decisions, and merge authority are
  recorded outside agent sessions. Checks are selected according to the
  artifact at risk, and documented incidents show their observed reach
  and limits.
  % CLAIM: CLM-005, CLM-007, CLM-008, CLM-009
\item An evidence method for research assisted by agents that combines
  session log escrow, claim-to-evidence verification, and cost
  reconciliation. It supports auditable statements about the project
  while making coverage gaps explicit. % CLAIM: CLM-002, CLM-003
\end{enumerate}

Section~\ref{sec:formalization} states the two capstone theorems.
Section~\ref{sec:workflow} presents the harness, and
Section~\ref{sec:incidents} examines four incidents at its boundaries.
Section~\ref{sec:discussion} gives recommendations and limitations.
