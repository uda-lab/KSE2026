\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

We reported a completed, kernel-only Lean~4 and mathlib formalization of
Leray--Hopf weak-solution existence on $\mathbb{T}^3$ and $\mathbb{R}^3$,
built over roughly six weeks under an agent-orchestrated development
regime, together with the workflow that produced it and an evidence-backed
account of four incidents in which a green build and a passing axiom check
were not sufficient to guarantee the campaign was proceeding soundly.
In each incident, the gap was closed by a mechanism external to the
build -- independent statement review backed by a concrete counterexample,
exhaustive declaration-level comparison against a prior known-good state,
or symptom-based resource monitoring -- and in each case the same evidence
also showed that mechanism's limits: a guardrail closed the specific gap
that exposed it, not the class of gap in general. The result is not a
claim that kernel checking is dispensable, but the opposite: kernel
checking is necessary and was never violated in the delivered capstones,
while being demonstrably insufficient, on its own, to have caught what
threatened the campaign's reliability along the way. Process-level
reliability management -- statement-first review, exhaustive comparison
against prior state, and resource-aware orchestration, each backed by
recorded, checkable evidence -- is therefore not an optional supplement to
proof checking in agent-orchestrated formalization, but a necessary and,
as this campaign shows, implementable part of it.
