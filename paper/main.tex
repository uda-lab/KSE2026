\title{Formalizing Leray--Hopf in Lean~4 with AI Agents:\\
Workflow, Incidents, and Recovery}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Anonymous Author(s)}
\IEEEauthorblockA{\todo{Affiliation --- check KSE 2026 double-blind policy before filling}}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Large language model agents can now sustain formalization efforts
spanning weeks and more than a hundred pull requests, which shifts the central
reliability question from proof search to process governance: a kernel
check verifies that a proved declaration is correct given its stated type,
but not that the type is what was intended, that it is still present
after a later refactor, or that a reported build success reflects genuine
on-disk state. We report a completed, kernel-only Lean~4 and mathlib
formalization of Leray--Hopf weak-solution existence for the homogeneous
Navier--Stokes equations on the 3-torus and on $\mathbb{R}^3$, developed
over roughly six weeks under an agent-orchestrated regime that consumed,
on evidence collected so far, at least 15.1M output tokens and 199.1
active hours. To our knowledge, no machine-checked existence theory for a
nonlinear fluid PDE has previously been reported in Lean, Coq, or
Isabelle/HOL. We present the workflow that produced this result --
statement-first development, independent adversarial review, and
machine-checked verification gates -- an evidence-backed account of four
incidents where build success and axiom checking alone were insufficient
to guarantee sound progress, and an evidence methodology combining
session-log escrow, machine-checked claim-to-evidence verification, and
cost accounting reconciled against actual billing records.
\end{abstract}
